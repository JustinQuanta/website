Understanding the Discount Rates Used:

The "Intrinsic Value" is an estimate of a stock's true value based on its expected future cash flows. To calculate this, future cash flows are discounted back to their present value using a "discount rate" (also known as the required rate of return).

  • Fixed Discount Rate (e.g., 7.50%): This is a standard, fixed percentage used for comparison. It can represent a general market expectation, a personal investment goal, or a benchmark rate.
  • Calculated Discount Rate (WACC): This rate is the Weighted Average Cost of Capital specific to the company. It represents the average minimum return the company must earn to satisfy its debt holders and equity investors. A common method to estimate a company's risk and expected return.
  • Custom Rate: Allows you to input your own desired rate of return for the valuation.

Key takeaway: A higher discount rate generally leads to a lower estimated intrinsic value because future earnings are considered less valuable in today's terms. Comparing values from different discount rates helps assess how sensitive the valuation is to this assumption.
