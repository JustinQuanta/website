Understanding the Model Parameters:

The "Intrinsic Value" is an estimate of a stock's true value based on its expected future cash flows. To calculate this, future cash flows are discounted back to their present value.

  • Projection Years: This is the 'finite horizon' — the number of years into the future we will forecast and sum up the cash flows. Unlike the WACC model, this method does not assume the company grows forever (no terminal value).
  • Discount Rate: Your personal required rate of return. A higher rate means future cash flows are considered less valuable today, leading to a lower intrinsic value.
  • Margin of Safety: A buffer or discount applied to the calculated intrinsic value to account for potential forecasting errors, providing a purchase price below the estimated fair value.
  • Growth Rate Used: For each scenario, the growth rate is chosen from analyst estimates (forward-looking growth forecasts) and the company’s historical equity growth. Conservative, moderate, and optimistic scenarios use the lowest, average, and highest rates respectively.

Key takeaway: The growth rates directly impact the intrinsic value calculation. The model transparently shows which growth rate was used for each scenario above, combining analyst forecasts and historical equity growth.
